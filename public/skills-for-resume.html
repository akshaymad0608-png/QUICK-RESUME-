Skills to Put on a Resume

The right skills, matched to the job description, are what ATS software and recruiters scan for first. Here’s what to include — and how.

Updated August 2026 · By QuickResume · Free · No sign-up

Quick answer

List a mix of hard skills (tools, languages, software) and a few relevant soft skills, and mirror the exact terms from the job description. Group them so they’re easy to scan.

Hard skills by role (examples)

  • Software: Java, Python, React, SQL, Git, AWS, REST APIs, data structures
  • Marketing: SEO, Google Analytics, content strategy, Meta Ads, email marketing
  • Data: SQL, Python, Excel, Power BI, Tableau, statistics, ETL
  • Finance/Accounts: Tally, advanced Excel, GST, financial modelling, reconciliation
  • Design: Figma, Adobe XD, prototyping, design systems, user research

Soft skills that matter

Communication, problem-solving, teamwork, ownership, adaptability and time management — but only list ones you can back up with examples in your experience section.

Always match your skills to the specific job posting. ATS software often scores resumes on keyword overlap with the job description.

Frequently asked questions

What skills should I put on my resume?
A mix of hard skills (tools, languages, software) and a few relevant soft skills, all matched to the specific job description.

How many skills should a resume have?
Around 8–15 relevant skills, grouped for easy scanning — quality and relevance beat a long, generic list.

Should I list soft skills?
A few, yes — but only ones you can demonstrate with examples in your experience. Recruiters value proof over buzzwords.
