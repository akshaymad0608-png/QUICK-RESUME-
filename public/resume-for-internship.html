Resume for Internship: What to Include as a Student

Internship recruiters know you are still studying. They are looking for proof that you learn quickly and have already tried things.

Updated October 2026 · By QuickResume · Free · No sign-up

Quick answer

Keep an internship resume to one page. Lead with education, then projects, then any experience, skills and activities. Pick details that match the internship, and keep the layout simple so it reads well on screen and in software.

Section order that works

  1. Header: name, phone, professional email, city, and a LinkedIn or GitHub link if it is tidy.
  2. Short objective (optional): one or two lines naming the internship field. See career objective examples.
  3. Education: course, college, expected graduation year, and CGPA or percentage if it helps you.
  4. Projects: two or three relevant ones, with what you did and what you used.
  5. Experience: any internship, part-time work, freelance work or volunteering.
  6. Skills: tools and languages you can genuinely use.
  7. Activities and certifications: clubs, competitions, courses.

What recruiters look for

Without work history, they usually look for relevance and initiative: a project in the right area, a skill that matches the listing, or a role where you took responsibility. Read the internship posting and mirror its wording where it is true for you. If it asks for Excel and you have used Excel, say so using that word.

Sample entries (fictional, for illustration)

Projects

  • Expense Tracker Web App: built a simple tracker using HTML, CSS and JavaScript; added filtering by month and saved data in the browser.
  • Campus Survey Analysis: collected responses from classmates, cleaned the data in Excel and presented the main findings in a short report.

Activities

  • Treasurer, college coding club: managed the event budget and tracked expenses for two events.

Notice the pattern: what you did, with what, and the result where you have one. Do not invent numbers. If you cannot measure it honestly, describe it plainly.

What to leave out

  • School marks from several years back, unless the listing asks for them.
  • Skill bars or percentages. They cannot be verified and do not parse well. A plain list is clearer.
  • Personal details like date of birth or marital status. These are not needed for an internship resume. For forms that ask for them, see the biodata format guide.
  • Padding. One strong page is better than two thin ones.

Frequently asked questions

Can I apply for an internship with no experience?
Yes, internships are meant for people still learning. Show projects, coursework, college activities and skills that relate to the role, and be honest about your level.

Should I add a cover letter for an internship?
If the posting asks for one, yes. Otherwise a short, tailored letter can help but is rarely required. See the cover letter guide for freshers.

How many projects should I list?
Two or three relevant ones are usually enough. Choose the ones closest to the internship over simply the most recent.
