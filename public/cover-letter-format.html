Cover Letter Format

A cover letter is a short, focused note that explains why you suit one specific job. This guide shows the standard format, what to write in each part, and a complete example you can adapt.

Updated October 2026 · By QuickResume · Free · No sign-up

Quick answer

A cover letter has four parts: header, opening paragraph, one or two body paragraphs, and a closing. Keep it to half to three-quarters of a page, tailored to the job, and use it to connect your experience to the employer's needs rather than repeat your resume.

Why the format matters

Hiring managers often read many applications in one sitting. A cover letter that is clearly organised and short is easy to read and signals that you can communicate well. A long, unstructured letter is easy to skip.

The format is not complicated. What makes a letter useful is that every paragraph has one job. Below is the structure, followed by a worked example.

Note that not every application asks for a cover letter. If an email application or job portal field is open, a short cover note is still a good way to introduce yourself. If the employer says it is optional, a good letter can still help you stand out from applicants who skipped it.

The four-part structure

  1. Header: your name, phone, email, city, date, then the employer's name and company. Address a real person if you can find one.
  2. Opening (2 to 3 lines): name the role and say, in one sentence, why you are a strong fit.
  3. Body (1 or 2 short paragraphs): match two or three of your strongest points to what the job asks for. Give specifics.
  4. Closing (2 lines): say you would welcome a conversation, thank them, and sign off politely.

That is the whole format. Everything else is detail.

Header and greeting

Put your details at the top, then the date, then the recipient. In an email, your signature can hold the contact details, so the header can be brief.

Greet by name when possible: “Dear Mr. Rao,” or “Dear Ms. Iyer,”. If the name is not given after a genuine search on the company page or LinkedIn, “Dear Hiring Manager,” is perfectly acceptable. Avoid “To whom it may concern” and “Dear Sir/Madam” where you can.

Write an opening that names the role

Do not begin with “I am writing to apply for the post advertised.” The reader already knows that. Say the role and give a reason you fit, in the same breath.

Weak: I am writing to express my interest in the position of Data Analyst at your company.

Better: I am applying for the Data Analyst role at Northwind Analytics. I have spent two years turning messy sales data into weekly dashboards that a regional team used to plan stock, and I would like to bring that work to your retail clients.

The second version tells the reader something about you in the first lines.

Build the body around their needs

Read the job post and pick the two or three requirements that matter most. For each, give one piece of evidence from your own work. Where possible, include a real number or a concrete outcome, but only if it is true.

A simple approach is to take each requirement and write one sentence on what you did, one on how, and one on the result. If you are a fresher, use projects, internships or college work in the same way. Our page on the cover letter for freshers has examples for that case.

Do not copy your resume line by line. The resume lists what you did; the letter explains why it matters for this employer.

A complete worked example (fictional)

Dear Mr. Rao,

I am applying for the Data Analyst role at Northwind Analytics. I have spent two years turning messy sales data into weekly dashboards that a regional team used to plan stock, and I would like to bring that work to your retail clients.

Your job post asks for strong SQL and clear reporting. At my current company, I wrote the SQL queries that replaced a manual Excel process, which saved the team roughly two hours every Monday. I also present the results to non-technical managers, so I am used to explaining numbers in plain language.

You also mention working with changing priorities. During a festive-season sale, I rebuilt our daily report in two days when the product categories were reorganised, and the team had accurate figures on time.

I would welcome a chance to discuss how I can support your analytics team. Thank you for your time and consideration.

Yours sincerely,
Meera Nair

This letter is about 170 words. It names the company and the job, connects three points to the posting, and ends clearly. All names and details here are invented for illustration.

Closing, tone and length

End with a calm, confident call to action: you would like to talk, and you are available. Avoid begging language (“kindly give me a chance”) and avoid demands (“call me immediately”).

Keep the tone professional and warm, in simple English. Short sentences are easier to read than long, formal ones. Use “Yours sincerely” if you named the person, or “Yours faithfully” or “Regards” otherwise. Both are acceptable in India.

For length, three to four short paragraphs and under one page is the standard. Send it as a PDF unless the portal asks for another format. Pairing it with a clean resume helps; see how to make a resume.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Sending the same letter to every employer without changing the company or role name.
  • Leaving another company's name in by mistake after copying a template.
  • Repeating the resume instead of adding context.
  • Starting every sentence with “I”.
  • Listing personal qualities with no proof, such as “I am hardworking and sincere”.
  • Typos in the recipient's name or company name.
  • Writing more than a page.

Proofread twice, once on screen and once aloud, before you hit send.

Before sending, read the first two lines: if they could be sent to any company, rewrite them to mention this role and one reason you fit.

Frequently asked questions

What is the correct cover letter format?
Header with your details and the recipient, an opening that names the role and your fit, one or two body paragraphs that match your experience to the job, and a short closing with a call to action.

How long should a cover letter be?
Usually three to four short paragraphs, between 150 and 300 words, and never more than one page.

Is a cover letter necessary in India?
Not always. Many portals only ask for a resume, but when the option exists, a short tailored letter can add context that a resume cannot.

Should I write the cover letter in the email body or attach it?
If you apply by email, a short version in the body works well, with the resume attached. If the portal asks for an upload, attach the letter as a PDF.

Can a fresher write a good cover letter without experience?
Yes. Use projects, internships, coursework and extracurricular work as evidence, and be specific about what you did and learned.
