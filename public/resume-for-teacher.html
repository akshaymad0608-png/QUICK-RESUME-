Teacher Resume

A strong teacher resume shows student outcomes, subject expertise and classroom skills. Here’s the format, skills and examples that stand out.

Updated August 2026 · By QuickResume · Free · No sign-up

Quick answer

Lead with a summary of your subject and level, then teaching experience with outcomes (results, engagement), qualifications (B.Ed/degrees), and skills (curriculum, assessment, EdTech). Keep it clean and ATS-friendly.

What to include

  1. Header — name, contact, and the subject/level you teach.
  2. Summary — years teaching, subjects, level (primary/secondary/college).
  3. Experience — schools, roles, and measurable outcomes (results, pass rates, engagement).
  4. Qualifications — B.Ed, degrees, TET/CTET or relevant certifications.
  5. Skills — curriculum design, assessment, classroom management, EdTech tools.

Strong bullet examples

  • Improved class average by 18% through structured revision and continuous assessment.
  • Introduced project-based learning that lifted student participation noticeably.
  • Mentored 30+ students; several placed in top competitive exams.
List certifications like B.Ed, TET/CTET clearly, and show student outcomes with numbers wherever possible.

Frequently asked questions

What should a teacher resume include?
A summary of your subject and level, teaching experience with measurable student outcomes, qualifications (B.Ed, TET/CTET), and skills like curriculum design and assessment.

What skills go on a teacher resume?
Curriculum design, lesson planning, classroom management, assessment, differentiated instruction, and EdTech tools — plus strong communication.

How do I show impact as a teacher?
Use numbers — improved class averages, pass rates, participation, or students placed in exams — rather than just listing duties.
