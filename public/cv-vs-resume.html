CV vs Resume: What's the Difference?

Two words for the same document, most of the time — except when they're not. Here's what actually changes.

Updated August 2026 · By QuickResume · Free · No sign-up

Quick answer

In India, the UK and most of the world, "CV" and "resume" mean the same 1-2 page document — job portals use the words interchangeably. The formal difference only matters for US academic, research, medical or government roles, where a CV is a longer, comprehensive career record with no page limit.

The short version

ResumeCV (formal / academic sense)
Length1-2 pagesNo fixed limit — grows with your career
ContentRelevant experience, tailored per jobFull history — every publication, talk, grant, role
Used forMost private-sector jobs, worldwideAcademia, research, medicine, government (mainly US)
Edited per application?Yes, tailored each timeRarely — sections are added over, not rewritten

Why the confusion

Outside the US, "CV" is simply the common word for what Americans call a resume — a short, tailored document listing your relevant experience. Job postings on Indian portals that ask for a "CV" are asking for exactly the same thing as one asking for a "resume": one to two pages, tailored to the role.

Inside the US academic and research world, the words split. There, a resume stays short and job-specific, while a CV is a complete, chronological record of your academic and professional life — every degree, publication, conference talk, grant and teaching role, with no length cap. Someone ten years into a research career might have a CV running six or eight pages, and that's normal for that context.

Which one do you need?

  • Applying for a job in India, on Naukri, LinkedIn or a company site: a standard resume — 1-2 pages, tailored to the role — whichever word the posting uses.
  • Applying to a US company outside academia: a resume, even if you personally call it a CV out of habit.
  • Applying for a US academic, research, postdoc or medical position: the formal CV — comprehensive, chronological, every credential included.
  • Not sure which the employer means? Send the shorter, tailored resume format unless the posting explicitly asks for "a full academic CV" or similar — that phrasing is the signal, not the word "CV" alone.
The safest rule: if you're not in US academia, build a resume — tailored, 1-2 pages — and call it whatever the job posting calls it. The document underneath doesn't need to change.

Build the document that actually gets read

Whatever you call it, the same rules apply: lead with your strongest, most relevant experience, use measurable achievements over vague duties, and keep the format clean enough for both a human and an ATS to scan quickly.

Frequently asked questions

Is CV and resume the same thing in India?
In everyday use on Indian job portals, yes — "CV" and "resume" are used interchangeably to mean the same 1-2 page document. The formal difference only really matters for academic, research or government roles, where a CV means the longer, chronological document described above.

Which is longer, a CV or a resume?
A resume is 1-2 pages. A CV (in its formal sense) has no fixed length — it lists your full academic and professional history and grows as your career does, often running several pages.

Should I title my document "CV" or "Resume"?
Match the job posting's wording. If it says "submit your CV" in India, the UK or most of Europe, a standard 1-2 page resume is almost always what's expected. For US academic, medical or research positions, "CV" means the longer formal document.
